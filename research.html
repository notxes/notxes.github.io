Graduate Research — In Progress

Network Segmentation &
Lateral Movement

Master's research examining the effects of VLAN segmentation and least-privilege firewall policies on credential-based lateral movement within a controlled Active Directory environment.

← Back to Projects Supporting Homelab
Research Overview

Research Question

To what extent does VLAN-based network segmentation with least-privilege inter-VLAN access control reduce successful credential-based lateral movement compared with a flatter network design in a controlled Active Directory cyber range?

Current Status

  • Research question and technical scope defined.
  • Existing lab infrastructure being documented.
  • Security Onion monitoring visibility being validated.
  • Experiment procedures and evidence collection in development.
  • Graduate research remains in progress.

Technical Environment

  • Proxmox virtualization infrastructure
  • Windows Server Active Directory
  • Windows 11 client systems
  • OPNsense firewall and VLAN segmentation
  • Security Onion, Zeek, and Suricata
  • Controlled Kali Linux testing environment
Research Methodology

Controlled Experimental Design

Baseline Condition

A flatter network design with broader communication paths between selected systems.

Experimental Condition

Segmented VLANs with explicitly configured least-privilege OPNsense firewall rules.

Attack Scenario

Controlled credential-based lateral movement, including Pass-the-Hash scenarios where appropriate.

Planned Measurements

  • Reachable network services
  • Successful and blocked movement attempts
  • Firewall allow and deny evidence
  • Detection and alert coverage
  • Detection latency
  • Windows authentication events
  • Zeek and Suricata network evidence

Repeatability

Standardized test procedures, restored snapshots, and repeated experimental runs.

Research Integrity

Scope & Ethical Boundaries

Testing Environment

All experimentation takes place within a controlled cybersecurity lab. No production municipal networks, organizational systems, or resident data are used.

Reporting & Results

Findings will be published after the completion of repeated testing and analysis. Results are limited to the defined lab environment and experimental conditions.

Research documentation and supporting files will be made available as they are completed, reviewed, and approved for public release.
